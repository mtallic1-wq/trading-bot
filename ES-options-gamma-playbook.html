INTRO Options Gamma for Beginners: How it Works

Read this first if you are new to Gamma Exposure (GEX) and Market Maker flows.

1. What is a Market Maker / Dealer?

When someone buys a Call option (a bet that the S&P 500 will go up) or a Put option (a bet that it will go down), they buy it from a Market Maker (dealer). The dealer does not want to take a directional bet—they make money on transaction fees. To protect themselves, they offset their risk using a process called Delta Hedging.

2. What is Delta Hedging & Gamma?

If a dealer sells you a Call option, they are short the market. To hedge, they must buy S&P 500 futures. As the price goes higher, the Call option becomes more likely to win, meaning the dealer must buy *more* futures to keep their hedge. The rate of change at which they must adjust their hedge is called Gamma.

Because market makers trade hundreds of billions of dollars, their hedging orders act as massive tide flows in the market, pushing prices around key options strikes.

3. The Two Gamma Regimes (Your Volatility Guide)

The total sum of market maker positioning determines the daily regime:

  • Positive Gamma (+GEX) Regime: Dealers are net "long gamma". To keep their books balanced, they must buy when price goes down and sell when price goes up. This counter-cyclical buying and selling suppresses volatility. The market behaves like it is moving through molasses, leading to range-bound days.
  • Negative Gamma (-GEX) Regime: Dealers are net "short gamma". To keep their books balanced, they must sell when price goes down and buy when price goes up. This pro-cyclical trading acts as a massive amplifier. Dips are met with intense dealer selling, causing swift market crashes and runaway trending runs.

4. Decoding the 4 Key Options Levels

  • Gamma Flip (Zero-Gamma Strike): The critical boundary level. If the S&P 500 spot price is above this level, the market is in a Positive Gamma (low vol) regime. If price breaks below it, we instantly shift to Negative Gamma (high vol). It acts as a major regime pivot.
  • Call Wall: The strike price with the largest concentration of positive call option gamma. As price approaches this level, dealers are forced to sell futures to hedge, making the Call Wall act as a major resistance ceiling.
  • Put Wall: The strike price with the largest concentration of put option gamma. As price falls here, dealers must buy futures to hedge, making it a firm support floor (in Positive Gamma). *Warning: In Negative Gamma, if the Put Wall breaks, it turns into resistance and accelerates the dump.*
  • 0DTE Magnet: The strike price with the highest same-day expiring option volume. Near the end of the trading day, options expire rapidly. Market makers must quickly unload their hedges, creating a gravitational pull that sucks the S&P 500 price directly to the magnet strike.
